HOW PLAGIGUARD WORKS

From submission to a clear, evidence-based similarity report.

PlagiGuard keeps every step visible. A student submits work, the browser prepares the text, the comparison engine measures shared n-grams, and the instructor reviews the evidence before marking.

THE PIPELINE

Six steps keep the comparison easy to understand.

The interface is designed to show what happened to the text and why the resulting similarity score appeared.

01
Step 1

Validate the submission

PlagiGuard counts the words first. Work below the instructor’s minimum is rejected before similarity analysis starts.

02
Step 2

Normalize and tokenize

Text is converted to lowercase, punctuation is normalized and the content is split into comparable word tokens.

03
Step 3

Build n-grams

Consecutive tokens are grouped into overlapping n-word phrases such as “javascript variables are” for comparison.

04
Step 4

Calculate Jaccard similarity

The engine compares the unique n-gram sets using intersection ÷ union and returns a percentage.

05
Step 5

Flag when appropriate

Each submission’s highest pairwise similarity is compared with the instructor-defined review threshold.

06
Step 6

Review and mark

The instructor inspects the matching evidence, adds context, records marks and leaves feedback for the student.

THE METHOD

Simple enough to explain in a viva, useful enough to support review.

PlagiGuard intentionally uses a transparent comparison method rather than hiding the score behind a black box. That makes the result easier to demonstrate, test and discuss in an academic project.

J(A, B) = |A ∩ B| / |A ∪ B|A and B are the unique n-gram sets extracted from two documents.
